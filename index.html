Drain Cleaning in Everett: A Clogged Drain Checklist

Most clogs build up over weeks before they stop a drain. Work through this list to tell a one-fixture clog from a main line problem.

Is it the kitchen sink?

Kitchen drains clog mostly from grease. It goes down as a liquid, then cools and hardens on the inside of the pipe, narrowing it a little more with every load of dishes. Coffee grounds, food scraps and produce stickers add to it.

Does the dishwasher back up too?

If water backs up in both the sink and the dishwasher, or the sink drains and then stops again within a day, the blockage is probably further down the line than a basic snake will reach.

Is it a bathroom sink or tub?

Bathroom drains clog from hair bound up with soap scum. It happens gradually, which is why people often wait longer than they should. A p-trap clean-out or a drain snake usually clears a single hair clog.

Are several fixtures slow on one floor?

That points to something further down the branch line, not one clogged trap.

Does the lowest drain act up first?

If a basement floor drain or a downstairs toilet backs up first, or water comes up in the tub when you flush, the clog is in the main line. That is cleared differently from a sink clog, and a root-choked side sewer is a common cause; see sewer line repair.

Skip the bottle of drain cleaner

Chemical openers rarely fix a clog that is past the trap, and they leave standing chemicals for whoever opens the drain next.
